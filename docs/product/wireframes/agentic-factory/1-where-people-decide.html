Design notes: one short list of what people decide, and everything else runs on its own
  1. 1 Govern’s Autonomy tab becomes Where people decide. It is the compliance boundary for the install: 7 kinds of decision, plus an 8th that is fixed (changing the list). Everything else runs on its own under the captain.
  2. 2 One multi-line input takes a rule in plain words (same pattern as Add a project). Helm reads it against the list, not a keyword match.
  3. 3 Here’s what I understood shows each change, flags the parts already handled (merging to staging branches), and replays the last 30 days so the effect shows before you apply it.
  4. 4 One primary, Apply. It asks for a passkey because changing the list is on the list.
  5. 5 Each card shows its default, scope (install or per project), who decides, the 24 h count and the reason in one line. A click opens a popover with the details and the audit trail. The cards follow the irreversible backstop in autonomy.py: a card may make a rule stricter and can never make it looser.
  6. 6 The monitoring agents, one card each. Each card shows what the agent watches and what it did alone vs. what waited. Security CVE and Infra host-health are new watchers (see AUDIT.md).
  7. 7 The last 24 h as lanes: teal = decided alone, green = fixed something, amber = waited for a person. Hover any mark for its one-line reason.
  8. 8 Any mark opens the decision as a diagram (signal → agent → on the list? → act → outcome), the same diagram as Agents at work. The prose stays behind the expand.
  9. 9 Click counts and the 21-row parity table: nothing is removed. Expert grids stay under More.
